# Comparison ("X vs Y") Article Template

## Target Audience & Expertise Level
- **Primary**: Engineers and tech leads choosing between specific technologies for a real project
- **Expertise**: Beginner-to-intermediate with at least one candidate; wants informed comparison, not marketing
- **Prerequisite knowledge**: Understands the problem domain both candidates address; may have tried one

## Recommended Word Count: 2000-4000 words

## Structure
1. **Hook** — Why this comparison matters now (market shift, new release, common confusion)
   - *Purpose*: Establish timeliness — a comparison only matters if the decision is live
   - *Transition*: From "why decide now" to "how we'll evaluate"
2. **Criteria** — What dimensions to evaluate (performance, DX, ecosystem, cost, etc.)
   - *Purpose*: Set the rubric upfront so the comparison feels systematic, not cherry-picked
   - *Transition*: From criteria to the first candidate
3. **Candidate A deep-dive** — Architecture, strengths, weaknesses, sweet spots
   - *Purpose*: Thorough, fair assessment — show where it shines AND where it struggles
   - *Transition*: From candidate A to "now let's look at the other side"
4. **Candidate B deep-dive** — Architecture, strengths, weaknesses, sweet spots
   - *Purpose*: Equal depth and fairness — readers will notice if one candidate gets more attention
   - *Transition*: From individual analysis to head-to-head
5. **Head-to-head comparison table** — Side-by-side across all criteria
   - *Purpose*: Scannable reference that readers will screenshot and share
   - *Transition*: From data to decision framework
6. **Decision framework** — When to pick each (team size, use case, constraints)
   - *Purpose*: The most valuable section — help readers map their situation to the right choice
   - *Transition*: From framework to verdict
7. **Verdict** — Opinionated recommendation with caveats
   - *Purpose*: Take a position — readers came here for guidance, not "it depends"

## Example Hooks
- "With the release of X v4.0 last month, the landscape has shifted. If you chose Y two years ago, it's time to re-evaluate."
- "I've deployed both X and Y in production for over a year each. The benchmarks tell one story. The on-call experience tells another."
- "Every week, someone on our team asks: should we use X or Y? Here's the decision framework we built after trying both."

## Tone
- Fair, evidence-based, "I've used both — here's what I found"
- Acknowledge that context matters — no universal winner
- Show don't tell — benchmarks, code samples, real trade-offs
- Opinionated in the verdict, balanced in the analysis

## Code Density: Medium (20-30%)

## Target Length: 2000-4000 words

## Anti-Patterns
- **Biased framing**: If readers can guess your preference from the criteria you chose, the comparison is rigged
- **Benchmark-only comparison**: Performance is one axis; DX, ecosystem, hiring, and maintenance matter too
- **Outdated information**: Check both candidates' latest versions — stale comparisons do real harm
- **Missing the comparison table**: Readers expect a scannable side-by-side; don't make them build it mentally
- **"It depends" without a framework**: Saying context matters is the start, not the end — provide the decision tree
- **Ignoring team factors**: Technology choice depends heavily on team size, experience, and hiring market

## Reader Promise
"After reading this, you'll know which to pick for YOUR situation"
