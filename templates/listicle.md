# Listicle ("N Things/Tools/Tips") Article Template

## Target Audience & Expertise Level
- **Primary**: Engineers looking for curated recommendations they can act on quickly
- **Expertise**: Varies by topic — adjust item depth to match; generally intermediate
- **Prerequisite knowledge**: Familiar with the domain; looking for vetted options, not introductions

## Recommended Word Count: 1500-3000 words

## Structure
1. **Hook** — Why this list, why now (pain point, trend, common request)
   - *Purpose*: Justify the curation — what problem does this list solve for the reader?
   - *Transition*: From "here's why you need this list" to the first item
2. **Items** — Each with subheading, explanation, concrete example
   - *Purpose*: The core value — each item is a self-contained recommendation with evidence
   - *Transition*: Each item ends with a one-line verdict or "best for" statement; natural flow between items
3. **Honorable mentions** — Quick shout-outs that didn't make the main list
   - *Purpose*: Show breadth of research and give readers more options to explore
   - *Transition*: From honorable mentions to decision guidance
4. **How to choose** — Decision criteria for picking from the list
   - *Purpose*: Help readers narrow from "all of these" to "the right one for me"

## Example Hooks
- "I tested 23 state management libraries so you don't have to. These 7 are the ones worth your time."
- "After mass-deleting 40% of our CI pipeline, these are the 5 practices that actually matter."
- "Every senior engineer I know has a personal toolkit. Here are the 10 tools that appear in almost all of them."

## Tone
- Punchy, scannable, "here's what you need to know"
- Each item stands alone — readers will skip around
- Lead each item with the takeaway, then support it
- Confident curation — you've done the research so the reader doesn't have to

## Code Density: Medium (15-30%)

## Target Length: 1500-3000 words

## Anti-Patterns
- **Padding the list**: Every item must earn its place — if you need filler to hit a number, make the list shorter
- **No ranking or grouping**: An unordered dump of items is less useful than one with tiers or categories
- **Missing concrete examples**: "Tool X is great" without a code sample or screenshot is just an endorsement
- **Clickbait count**: Choose the number based on what you found, not what sounds good — "7" is not always better than "5"
- **No selection criteria**: A list without a "how to choose" section leaves readers overwhelmed
- **All items same depth**: Lead items deserve more detail; later items can be shorter

## Reader Promise
"After reading this, you'll have a curated shortlist"
