# Narrative / War Story Article Template

## Target Audience & Expertise Level
- **Primary**: Engineers at all levels who learn through stories and want to avoid others' mistakes
- **Expertise**: Any level (the narrative carries the reader; technical depth is woven in naturally)
- **Prerequisite knowledge**: General engineering awareness; specific tech knowledge is introduced in context

## Recommended Word Count: 1500-3000 words

## Structure
1. **Hook** — The moment everything changed / went wrong
   - *Purpose*: Drop the reader into the most dramatic or pivotal moment — in medias res
   - *Transition*: From the crisis moment to "let me back up and explain how we got here"
2. **Setup** — The situation, the team, the constraints
   - *Purpose*: Build context so the reader understands the stakes and constraints
   - *Transition*: From "here's the world we were in" to "then this happened"
3. **Crisis** — What happened, why it was hard
   - *Purpose*: Escalate tension — show the specific moment things broke and why it mattered
   - *Transition*: From "we had a problem" to "here's what we tried"
4. **Journey** — What you tried, what failed, what you learned
   - *Purpose*: The longest section — honest account of dead ends, wrong assumptions, partial fixes
   - *Transition*: From struggle to the breakthrough
5. **Key Insight** — The breakthrough moment
   - *Purpose*: The "aha" that made everything click — this is the emotional and intellectual peak
   - *Transition*: From insight to resolution
6. **Resolution** — How it turned out
   - *Purpose*: Show the concrete outcome — numbers, before/after, team impact
   - *Transition*: From specific outcome to general principle
7. **Takeaway** — The lesson others can apply
   - *Purpose*: Distill the story into a portable principle the reader can use in their own work

## Example Hooks
- "At 2:47 AM on a Tuesday, our database started returning results from three years in the future. This is the story of why."
- "We were 48 hours from launch when I realized our entire caching strategy was based on an assumption that had never been true."
- "The fix was a single line of code. Finding that line took our team six weeks."

## Tone
- Personal, engaging, "let me tell you what happened"
- Strong narrative arc — tension, discovery, resolution
- Vulnerability — admit mistakes, share the messy middle
- Specific sensory details — what time it was, what you were looking at, what the error message said

## Code Density: Low-Medium (15-25%)

## Target Length: 1500-3000 words

## Anti-Patterns
- **Humble-brag disguised as war story**: The point is the lesson, not how smart you are for solving it
- **Missing the messy middle**: Jumping from problem to solution without showing the wrong turns destroys credibility
- **Vague stakes**: "It was a big problem" means nothing — give specific numbers, deadlines, user impact
- **No transferable lesson**: A story without a generalizable takeaway is just a diary entry
- **Too many characters**: Keep the cast small — readers lose track of team members beyond 3-4 named people
- **Sanitizing the failure**: Readers connect with honest accounts of mistakes, not polished hero narratives

## Reader Promise
"After reading this, you'll think differently about X"
