# Opinion / Hot Take Article Template

## Target Audience & Expertise Level
- **Primary**: Engineers and tech leaders who care about industry direction and best practices
- **Expertise**: Intermediate-to-advanced (has enough experience to evaluate the argument)
- **Prerequisite knowledge**: Familiar with the technology or practice being discussed; has their own opinions

## Recommended Word Count: 1000-2500 words

## Structure
1. **Thesis** — State your position clearly and boldly
   - *Purpose*: Lead with the conclusion — no burying the lede; readers should know your stance in the first paragraph
   - *Transition*: From the bold claim to "here's why this matters"
2. **Why this matters** — Stakes and context
   - *Purpose*: Explain what happens if the industry keeps doing it wrong (or right) — raise the stakes
   - *Transition*: From stakes to evidence
3. **Evidence** — Data, experience, examples supporting your view
   - *Purpose*: Build the case with specific, verifiable evidence — not vibes
   - *Transition*: From "here's my proof" to "I hear the counterargument"
4. **Steel man** — The strongest counterargument, honestly presented
   - *Purpose*: Show intellectual honesty by presenting the best version of the opposing view
   - *Transition*: From the counterargument to your rebuttal
5. **Rebuttal** — Why you still hold your position
   - *Purpose*: Address the counterargument head-on and explain why your position survives it
   - *Transition*: From rebuttal to practical implications
6. **Implications** — What this means for the reader
   - *Purpose*: Make it concrete — what should the reader do differently tomorrow?
   - *Transition*: From implications to call to action
7. **Call to action** — What should change
   - *Purpose*: End with a clear, specific ask — not a vague "think about it"

## Example Hooks
- "We need to stop treating microservices as the default architecture. For 90% of teams, they're actively harmful."
- "The 'clean code' movement has mass-produced codebases that are easy to read and impossible to change."
- "Your team doesn't have a technical debt problem. You have a decision-making problem."

## Tone
- Opinionated, confident, not aggressive
- Acknowledge complexity while maintaining a clear position
- Respect opposing views while arguing yours
- Conversational but rigorous — this is an argument, not a rant

## Code Density: Low (10-20%)

## Target Length: 1000-2500 words

## Anti-Patterns
- **Weak steel man**: If you can't state the counterargument in a way your opponents would endorse, you haven't earned the right to dismiss it
- **Rant without evidence**: Passion without data is just noise — every claim needs backing
- **False balance**: Having an opinion means picking a side; don't retreat to "it depends" in the conclusion
- **Straw man attacks**: Attacking a weak version of the opposing view makes you look lazy, not right
- **No stakes**: If nobody would disagree with your take, it's not an opinion piece — it's a truism
- **Missing the "so what"**: An opinion without implications or a call to action is an essay, not a catalyst

## Reader Promise
"After reading this, you'll have a strong opinion about X (even if you disagree)"
